    Institution Engagement Guide

    This document provides a structured approach to engaging research institutions, governments, universities, multilateral organizations, and other stakeholders in GLAPAGOS.

    This is not a marketing document. The credibility of GLAPAGOS depends on the quality of its work and governance. This guide focuses on creating conditions under which serious organizations find participation professionally valuable.

    Credibility Prerequisites

    Before active outreach, the following must be in place. Attempting to recruit institutions without them will undermine long-term credibility.

    Technical prerequisites: - Repository is publicly available and demonstrates serious engineering - CI/CD pipelines are operational and all checks pass - At least one functional software artifact is released - Documentation is complete and accurate - Security policy is in place

    Governance prerequisites: - Constitution and Governance Framework are published - Ethics and Governance Board composition process is documented - Conflict of interest policy is documented and enforced - Financial transparency policy is documented

    Research prerequisites: - At least one substantive technical output published - Publication policy is documented - Research ethics process is documented

    Do not skip these prerequisites. Institutions asked to join before these are in place will, and should, decline.

    Engagement by Institution Type

    Research Universities

    What they need: Credible research outputs, legitimate governance, publication opportunities, student and faculty engagement pathways.

    Approach: - Identify the relevant research center or department - Contact the research center director or relevant faculty, not university administration - Propose a specific, concrete form of engagement - Send documentation a researcher would find credible: the technical white paper, architecture document, and governance framework - Do not lead with membership fees. Lead with research value.

    Government Bodies

    What they need: Clear policy relevance, legal compatibility with their mandate, governance representing their interests without compromising sovereignty, low administrative burden.

    Approach: - Identify the relevant ministry (technology, digital transformation, science) - Contact at director or policy specialist level initially - Frame participation in terms of policy outcomes - Emphasize the non-binding nature of observer participation - Be explicit about sovereignty provisions in the Constitution

    Multilateral Organizations

    IDB: Active AI and digital economy programming. Target the Digital Economy Division and Innovation Laboratory. Frame around compute infrastructure investment and AI governance standards.

    UNDP Digital 4 Development Hub LAC: Explicitly coordinates inclusive digitalization. Target the Hub coordinator directly. Frame around their country office mandate.

    CAF: Co-funded LATAM-GPT. Has infrastructure investment capacity. Target the innovation or digital economy team.

    Civil Society Organizations

    What they need: Mission alignment, trustworthy governance, low financial barriers, genuine voice in decisions.

    Approach: - Waive or substantially reduce membership fees - Ensure Working Groups genuinely include civil society perspectives - Be explicit that civil society participation is substantive

    The Outreach Sequence

    For any institution, the recommended sequence is:

    1. Desk research: Understand the institution's mission, current AI activities, and relevant staff before contacting them.

    2. Initial contact: A concise, specific communication from a named individual with a relevant credential. Include specific reasons why this institution's participation would be valuable and what GLAPAGOS offers them.

    3. Documentation sharing: Share technical and governance documentation.

    4. Meeting or call: Focus on their questions and concerns.

    5. Concrete next step: Propose a specific, low-commitment engagement. Not membership. One Working Group meeting, one document review, one benchmark co-authorship.

    6. Membership discussion: After concrete engagement has demonstrated value, discuss formal membership.

    Building Research Credibility

    The fastest path to credibility is publishing work that researchers find valuable.

    Benchmark releases: A well-designed benchmark for multilingual AI evaluation will be cited and used. It establishes expertise without requiring large compute.

    Dataset contributions: A documented, governed dataset with clear provenance fills a gap researchers can point to.

    Technical reports: Analysis of AI governance frameworks across the hemisphere, comparison of regulatory approaches, or documentation of AI deployment challenges in specific sectors.

    Open workshops: Invite relevant researchers to a workshop focused on a specific technical problem. Publish the outputs.

    Community Health Metrics

    Track and publish annually: - Member institutions by country and category - Individual contributors by country and domain - Research outputs published - Working Group participation (unique attendees, affiliation diversity) - Benchmark adoptions by external researchers - Dataset downloads - Governance decision response times - Ethics and Governance Board findings and responses

    These metrics demonstrate institutional health to potential partners and funders. They also identify problems early.
