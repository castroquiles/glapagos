# AI Bill of Rights for the Americas
## Draft Framework for Public Comment

Status: Draft | Working Group: WG-GOV | Version: 0.1.0

This document is a working draft published for community comment.
It is not yet adopted policy. To comment, open a GitHub Issue with
label `wg-gov` or attend WG-GOV monthly meetings.

The framework draws on the U.S. Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights,
the EU AI Act, UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of AI, and
H.Res. 649 (118th Congress), adapted for the Western Hemisphere.

---

## Preamble

The nations and peoples of the Western Hemisphere affirm the following
rights as applicable to all persons interacting with or affected by AI
systems in the Americas.

These rights are principles for voluntary adoption and incorporation
into national and regional regulatory frameworks. They are not
self-executing legal obligations.

---

## Right 1: Safe and Effective Systems

You should be protected from unsafe or ineffective AI systems.

AI systems should be designed, developed, and deployed to be safe and
effective for their intended use. Before deployment in consequential
contexts, AI systems should be tested to verify they perform as intended
without undue risk.

Americas context: Testing must include evaluation on Spanish,
Portuguese, French, Haitian Creole, and other widely spoken hemisphere
languages. Systems affecting Indigenous communities must be evaluated
for cultural safety in those communities' contexts.

---

## Right 2: Algorithmic Non-Discrimination

You should not face discrimination from AI systems.

AI systems must not discriminate based on race, color, ethnicity,
national origin, language, gender, age, disability, religion,
socioeconomic status, geographic location, or other protected
characteristics, including through ostensibly neutral criteria that
produce disparate effects.

Americas context: The significant demographic diversity of the Americas,
including Indigenous, Afro-descendant, immigrant, and economically
marginalized communities, creates specific discrimination risks. AI in
consequential domains must be evaluated for disparate impact.

---

## Right 3: Transparency and Notice

You should know when AI is being used in decisions affecting you.

Where AI makes or substantially contributes to a decision affecting an
individual's rights, opportunities, or access to services, the individual
should be informed that AI was used, what it was designed to do, and
what data was used.

Americas context: Disclosure must be in a language the affected person
understands. English-only disclosure is insufficient where the affected
population primarily speaks other languages.

---

## Right 4: Explanation

You should be able to understand decisions affecting you.

Where AI substantially contributes to a consequential decision, the
affected individual has the right to a meaningful plain-language
explanation of how that decision was reached.

---

## Right 5: Human Alternatives

You should not be denied access to a human decision-maker.

AI systems should not eliminate entirely the availability of human
review in consequential decisions. Individuals who prefer human review
should be able to access it within a reasonable time.

---

## Right 6: Data Privacy and Autonomy

You should have control over your data.

Individuals have the right to know what data is collected about them,
for what purposes, to access and correct that data, and to request
deletion where there is no overriding public interest in retention.

---

## Right 7: Contestability

You have rights regarding automated decisions.

Where automated systems make decisions with no meaningful human
involvement, affected individuals have the right to know the decision
was automated, receive an explanation, contest it through a human
review process, and seek recourse for harm.

---

## Right 8: Human Oversight

AI systems in the public interest require meaningful human oversight.

AI deployed by governments or in public services must operate under
oversight where humans can monitor behavior, intervene when AI acts
inconsistently with its intended purpose or applicable law, and are
accountable for consequences.

---

## Implementation Framework

Pre-deployment assessment: Developers and deployers should conduct and
publish an assessment covering intended use, affected populations, data
used, known limitations, evaluation results, and planned monitoring.

Ongoing monitoring: Deployed systems should be monitored for
performance, disparate impact, and unexpected behavior.

Incident reporting: Significant AI incidents should be reported and
investigated. GLAPAGOS maintains a voluntary incident registry.
