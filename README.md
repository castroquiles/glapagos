## What GLAPAGOS Is

GLAPAGOS is an open-source infrastructure platform and institutional
framework designed to coordinate AI research, governance, and applied
development across the Americas. It is not a product company, a
competitive entry in the AI market, or a replacement for any existing
organization. It is shared infrastructure: a permanent, neutral,
multilateral institution that reduces coordination costs and increases
the quality of AI development across the hemisphere.

The analogy to CERN is structural. CERN succeeded because it provided
shared infrastructure that no single nation could build alone, neutral
governance that kept political interests subordinate to scientific ones,
and open publication norms that prevented the concentration of knowledge.
GLAPAGOS applies that model to AI: shared compute, open models, federated
governance, transparent research, and a common policy framework across
35-plus nations.

The platform operates at glapagos.com and glapagos.ai and serves as the
primary destination for all contributors, institutions, researchers,
developers, policymakers, and international organizations participating
in this initiative.

## Policy Foundation

- H.Res. 649 (118th Congress): U.S. House resolution urging a safe and
  responsible regional AI strategy for the Western Hemisphere, protecting
  civil rights, civil liberties, privacy, equal opportunity, and access
  to critical services.

- GENIA Americas / LATAM 4.0: Prior institutional work establishing a
  regional AI strategy, AI diplomacy framework, and the concept of an
  inter-American network of AI laboratories.

- LATAM-GPT (CENIA, Chile): Open-source foundation model coordinated
  across more than 60 institutions, establishing precedent for open
  hemispheric AI collaboration.

- UNDP Digital 4 Development Hub LAC: UN regional infrastructure for
  inclusive digital transformation.

- Inter-American Development Bank (IDB): Primary multilateral financing
  institution for the region and target institutional partner.

## Core Domains

1. Research: Open AI research with regional relevance, published without embargo
2. Governance: Regulatory frameworks, interoperability standards, policy toolkits
3. Safety: Evaluation methods, audit tools, incident reporting infrastructure
4. Foundation Models: Open training, multilingual models, cultural alignment
5. Compute Infrastructure: Federated compute access, resource sharing protocols
6. Education: Workforce development, AI literacy, institutional capacity
7. Civic Technology: Government applications, public-interest AI deployments
