# GLAPAGOS Citizenship Framework

Version 1.0

"Citizenship" in this context is not a legal or political status. It is
the term GLAPAGOS uses to describe the set of rights, responsibilities,
and recognition accorded to participants in the platform, by analogy to
the way citizenship in a republic confers both standing and obligation.

---

## 1. Participation Tiers

### 1.1 Observer

Who qualifies: Any individual or institution that has registered with
GLAPAGOS and agreed to the Code of Conduct.

Rights:
- Access to all public documentation and repositories
- Participation in public Working Group meetings
- Posting to community discussion forums
- Subscription to all public communications

Responsibilities:
- Compliance with the Code of Conduct
- Honest representation of affiliation and interests

How to obtain: Register at https://www.glapagos.com

---

### 1.2 Contributor

Who qualifies: Any individual who has made at least three substantive
contributions to GLAPAGOS in the past 12 months. Contributions include:
merged pull requests, accepted datasets, peer review of research outputs,
translation of official documents, completion of a documented Working
Group task, or production of accepted education materials.

Rights:
- All Observer rights
- Voting rights in Working Group decisions
- Eligibility to serve as Working Group lead
- Listed in the GLAPAGOS Contributors Registry
- GLAPAGOS Contributor credential for professional recognition

Responsibilities:
- Maintaining the quality of contributions
- Participating in peer review when asked
- Disclosing conflicts of interest
- Supporting onboarding of new contributors

How to obtain: Tracked automatically. Status issued quarterly.

---

### 1.3 Member Institution

Who qualifies: Any university, research institution, government body,
intergovernmental organization, civil society organization, or private
sector organization that has applied and been accepted, agreed to the
Membership Agreement, and demonstrated substantive commitment.

Rights:
- All Contributor rights for designated individuals
- Voting rights in the General Assembly
- Eligibility to nominate candidates for GLAPAGOS bodies
- Access to institutional collaboration programs
- Participation in federated compute allocation
- Formal designation as a GLAPAGOS Member Institution

Responsibilities:
- Payment of membership dues per the fee schedule
- Annual report on activities and contributions
- Compliance with all GLAPAGOS policies
- Designation of a primary institutional contact

How to apply: Via https://www.glapagos.com
Fees are waived or reduced for institutions in lower-income countries.

---

### 1.4 Fellow

Who qualifies: Individual researchers, practitioners, or policy
specialists who have made exceptional contributions and whose ongoing
engagement is of particular value. Selected by the Technical Council
or Ethics and Governance Board.

Rights:
- Formal recognition as a GLAPAGOS Fellow
- Advisory role in relevant Working Groups
- Priority access to GLAPAGOS research infrastructure
- Participation in the annual Fellows convening

Responsibilities:
- One substantive contribution per quarter
- Availability for consultation within their expertise
- Maintenance of conflict of interest disclosures

---

### 1.5 Partner Organization

Organizations with a formal collaboration agreement for a specific
purpose. Rights and responsibilities defined in the specific agreement.

## 3. Certification Programs

### Track 1: Responsible AI Development
Covers: Bias evaluation, safety testing, documentation standards,
ethical reasoning frameworks.
Audience: Developers, data scientists, ML engineers.

### Track 2: AI Governance and Policy
Covers: Regulatory frameworks, governance design, rights frameworks,
impact assessment.
Audience: Policy specialists, government officials, civil society.

### Track 3: Multilingual AI Systems
Covers: Low-resource language NLP, cultural adaptation, multilingual
evaluation.
Audience: Researchers and engineers working on language AI.

### Track 4: AI Safety Assessment
Covers: Red-teaming methodology, model evaluation, incident analysis,
reporting standards.
Audience: Safety researchers, auditors.

All certification materials are available in English and Spanish.
Certifications are valid for three years and require renewal through
demonstrated continued engagement.

## 4. Rights of All Participants

Right to Information: Access to all public governance documents,
decisions, and their rationales.

Right to Participate: Opportunity to contribute in proportion to tier,
without discrimination based on country of origin, language, gender,
race, ethnicity, religion, disability status, or sexual orientation.

Right to Attribution: Appropriate credit for substantive contributions.

Right to Appeal: The ability to appeal decisions affecting them through
the defined appeals process.

Right to Exit: The ability to withdraw at any time. Contributions
already made remain under their open licenses.

Right to Safe Participation: An environment free from harassment,
coercion, and discrimination as defined in the Code of Conduct.

---

## 5. Responsibilities of All Participants

Honesty: Accurate representation of qualifications, affiliations,
and interests.

Integrity: Compliance with scientific integrity standards.

Disclosure: Timely disclosure of conflicts of interest.

Constructiveness: Engaging with disagreements through reasoned argument.

Care: Treating fellow participants with respect per the Code of Conduct.
