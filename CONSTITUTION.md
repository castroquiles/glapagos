# GLAPAGOS Constitution
## Global Laboratory for AI Progress and Governance: Open Systems

Version 1.0 | Status: Draft for Ratification

---

## Preamble

The peoples and institutions of the Western Hemisphere, recognizing that
artificial intelligence is among the most consequential technological
developments in recorded history, and that its trajectory will be shaped
by the institutions, standards, and norms established in this founding
period, hereby establish this Constitution as the foundational instrument
of GLAPAGOS.

This Constitution is grounded in the following observations:

First, that the development of AI in the Americas has proceeded in a
fragmented manner, with insufficient coordination between nations,
disciplines, and sectors, and that this fragmentation produces worse
outcomes than a coordinated approach would.

Second, that the distribution of AI capabilities and benefits across the
hemisphere is deeply unequal, and that this inequality is the product of
institutional choices that can be made differently.

Third, that the governance of AI requires multilateral frameworks that do
not yet exist in sufficient form for this hemisphere.

Fourth, that open, shared infrastructure for AI development, comparable
to what CERN provides for particle physics, would significantly accelerate
beneficial AI development while reducing duplicated effort and
concentrations of control.

Fifth, that the nations of the Western Hemisphere share sufficient common
interests, values, and policy goals to make such coordination both
feasible and desirable.

## Article I: Name, Nature, and Mission

Section 1.1 Name
This institution is named GLAPAGOS: Global Laboratory for AI Progress
and Governance: Open Systems. Its primary online presence is maintained
at glapagos.com and glapagos.ai.

Section 1.2 Nature
GLAPAGOS is an open, multilateral, non-proprietary institution. It does
not seek profit, does not hold proprietary rights to research produced
under its auspices, and does not seek political power or regulatory
authority over any jurisdiction.

Section 1.3 Mission
The mission of GLAPAGOS is to coordinate artificial intelligence
research, governance, safety, and applied development across the Americas
in service of the public interest, and to provide open infrastructure
that enables researchers, developers, policymakers, and communities
across the hemisphere to participate in and benefit from AI development.

Section 1.4 Scope
GLAPAGOS operates across North America, Central America, South America,
and the Caribbean. It welcomes participation from institutions and
individuals worldwide, but its primary mandate concerns the Western
Hemisphere.

## Article II: Foundational Principles

Section 2.1 Openness
All research, code, data, and governance processes conducted under
GLAPAGOS auspices must be open by default. Exceptions require explicit
justification and are time-limited.

Section 2.2 Neutrality
GLAPAGOS does not take positions on partisan political matters. Its
infrastructure and governance are designed to prevent capture by any
single nation, company, institution, or ideological interest.

Section 2.3 Inclusion
GLAPAGOS is designed to include participation from historically
underrepresented communities: Indigenous communities, Afro-descendant
communities, rural populations, women and girls, speakers of minority
languages, and communities in lower-income nations. Inclusion is an
engineering constraint, not a statement of aspiration.

Section 2.4 Safety
GLAPAGOS treats AI safety as a core technical and governance concern.
All research and development conducted under GLAPAGOS auspices must
comply with the Safety Framework established in the Governance documents.

Section 2.5 Accountability
All decisions of GLAPAGOS bodies are documented, published, and subject
to community review. Financial accounts are publicly audited annually.

Section 2.6 Sovereignty Without Subordination
GLAPAGOS respects the sovereignty of member nations and does not seek to
override national law or policy. Participation is always voluntary.

Section 2.7 Scientific Integrity
Research conducted under GLAPAGOS auspices follows the highest standards
of scientific integrity, including open data, replication requirements,
and disclosure of all conflicts of interest.

## Article III: Institutional Structure

Section 3.1 Overview
GLAPAGOS is governed by four principal bodies:

a) The General Assembly: supreme deliberative body
b) The Executive Secretariat: day-to-day operations
c) The Technical Council: research and engineering oversight
d) The Ethics and Governance Board: independent permanent oversight

Section 3.2 The General Assembly
Composition: One delegate per member institution in good standing.
Authority: Constitutional amendments, strategic direction, budget
approval, election of senior officers, ratification of major agreements.
Quorum: Participation from institutions representing at least three of
the four sub-regions (North, Central, South, Caribbean).
Meetings: Annually in person, additional sessions by videoconference.
All sessions are recorded and transcripts are published.

Section 3.3 The Executive Secretariat
Led by a Secretary-General elected by the General Assembly for a
four-year term, renewable once, plus not more than four Deputy
Secretaries-General. Manages operations and represents GLAPAGOS
externally.

Section 3.4 The Technical Council
Elected representatives from each domain working group plus three
at-large members. Sets research priorities, approves technical
standards, manages the open model registry, and oversees compute
resource allocation. No single institution may hold more than two seats.

Section 3.5 The Ethics and Governance Board
Seven independent members serving staggered four-year terms. Members
must not hold positions in any member institution during their tenure.
Funded by a dedicated reserve that cannot be defunded by any other
GLAPAGOS body. Findings are advisory but must be formally responded to.

## Article IV: Membership

Section 4.1 Categories
a) Research Universities and Academic Institutions
b) National Governments and Intergovernmental Organizations
c) Non-governmental Organizations and Civil Society Organizations
d) Private Sector Organizations (subject to conflict-of-interest rules)
e) Individual Contributors (non-voting, recognized for contributions)

Section 4.2 Admission
Institutions apply to the Executive Secretariat, agree to the Code of
Conduct and Governance Framework, and demonstrate genuine commitment
to the mission.

Section 4.3 Obligations
Member institutions contribute to the commons, respect the Foundational
Principles, participate in governance, and comply with the Code of
Conduct.

Section 4.4 Suspension and Expulsion
Requires a two-thirds vote of the General Assembly, preceded by a
documented investigation and a formal opportunity to respond.

---

## Article V: Financial Structure

Section 5.1 Funding Sources
a) Membership dues structured by institution type and financial capacity
b) Grants from governments, development banks, and foundations
c) Contracts for research and technical assistance
d) Voluntary contributions from private sector partners

Section 5.2 Prohibitions
GLAPAGOS does not accept funding that restricts research publication,
grants donors preferential governance access, or comes from anonymous
sources above de minimis amounts.

Section 5.3 Transparency
All funding sources and amounts are published annually. The annual
financial audit is conducted by an independent firm and published.

Section 5.4 No Single-Donor Dependency
No single donor or donor category may provide more than twenty-five
percent of the annual operating budget.

---

## Article VI: Intellectual Property

Section 6.1 Default to Open
All research outputs, code, models, and data produced with GLAPAGOS
resources are open by default: Apache 2.0 (code), CC BY 4.0
(documentation), ODbL (data).

Section 6.2 Patent Policy
GLAPAGOS does not seek patents. Contributions carry a perpetual,
irrevocable patent grant consistent with the Apache 2.0 license.

---

## Article VII: Amendment

Amendments may be proposed by any member institution with support of
five additional member institutions. Published for community comment
for not less than sixty days. Adopted by a three-quarters supermajority
of the General Assembly with participation from at least three of the
four sub-regions. Effective thirty days after adoption.

---

## Article VIII: Entry into Force

This Constitution enters into force when ratified by member institutions
from at least five countries across at least two sub-regions of the
Western Hemisphere.
