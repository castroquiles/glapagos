# GLAPAGOS Manifesto

A Statement of Principles and Purpose

---

## Why This Exists

In 2026, the development of artificial intelligence is concentrated in a
small number of organizations in a small number of countries. This is not
an inherent property of the technology. It is a consequence of
institutional choices, investment patterns, and coordination failures
that can be addressed.

The Western Hemisphere contains approximately 1.2 billion people,
extraordinary diversity of language and culture, and a long institutional
tradition of multilateral cooperation. It contains leading research
universities, a rapidly growing developer community, governments that
have demonstrated capacity for technology policy, and communities with
urgent needs that AI could help address.

What it lacks is shared infrastructure. Not physical infrastructure,
which is being built. The institutional infrastructure: shared standards,
shared governance, shared research norms, shared compute access, and a
common framework for asking what AI should do and for whom.

GLAPAGOS exists to provide that infrastructure.

---

## The CERN Model

When European nations agreed in 1954 to create CERN, they were not naive
about the difficulty of getting governments to cooperate. They chose to
build shared physical infrastructure because shared infrastructure creates
shared interests. Nations that have invested in the infrastructure have
reasons to make the institution work.

They also chose to make CERN outputs fully open. Every discovery, every
paper, every dataset is available to any researcher anywhere. This was
not altruism. It was a recognition that open publication accelerates
science, that the prestige of open science attracts talent, and that
closed science at the frontier creates incentives for secrecy that slow
everyone down.

CERN has produced twelve Nobel Prizes. It invented the World Wide Web,
which it gave away. Its budget is smaller than most major tech companies'
annual research spending. Its impact per dollar is substantially greater.

GLAPAGOS applies this model to AI: shared open infrastructure, governed
by its participants, with outputs that belong to everyone.

The comparison is structural, not scale-equivalent. We are describing a
model of institutional design, not making claims about equivalent
resources or immediate impact.

## What AI Policy in the Americas Actually Requires

H.Res. 649 of the 118th Congress called for a regional AI strategy for
the Western Hemisphere that would protect civil rights and civil
liberties, ensure equal opportunity and access to AI-driven services,
and promote democratic values. This resolution reflects a real gap:
there is no such regional strategy.

The gap is not for want of interest. It is for want of institutional
infrastructure. Nations cannot cooperate on AI governance without shared
standards to cooperate around. Researchers cannot collaborate without
shared infrastructure to collaborate through. Developers cannot build for
the hemisphere without shared tools calibrated to the hemisphere's
languages, cultures, and contexts.

GLAPAGOS is not the regional AI strategy itself. It is the technical and
institutional substrate on which such a strategy can be built and
maintained.

---

## What We Are Not Claiming

We are not claiming that GLAPAGOS will close the AI gap between the
Americas and leading AI nations in the near term.

We are not claiming that hemispheric AI coordination is easy or that
political differences among member nations will not create friction.

We are not claiming that open-source approaches will always produce
models of comparable quality to large proprietary models.

We are claiming that:

- Coordination reduces duplicated effort and increases quality for the
  resources available
- Open governance prevents the capture of regional AI infrastructure by
  any single interest
- Shared standards reduce the cost of interoperability across
  jurisdictions
- Indigenous and minority language AI development will not happen at
  scale without deliberate institutional support
- The governance of AI in the Americas requires institutions that
  represent the Americas

These are not speculative claims. They are grounded in the documented
experience of international scientific cooperation and in the observed
failures of AI development to represent regional needs.

---

## Our Obligations

The institutions that sign this Manifesto take on the following
obligations:

To make our AI research open, unless there is a documented reason it
cannot be.

To support governance processes that represent the communities affected
by AI, not only the communities that build it.

To treat AI safety as a shared technical problem, not a competitive
differentiator.

To invest in multilingual and culturally representative AI, recognizing
that a model trained predominantly on English-language data is not a
universal tool.

To publish our failures as well as our successes, because reproducible
science requires both.

To acknowledge conflicts of interest and to recuse where they are
material.

To build for the long term.

## The Name

The Galapagos Islands are a symbol of something specific: the realization,
documented by careful observation in an isolated place, that the same
underlying principles produce radically different adaptations when applied
in different environments.

Darwin's insight was that diversity is not aberration from a norm. It is
the natural product of the interaction between common principles and
specific environments.

AI development in the Americas will, if it works, produce something
similar: AI systems adapted to the specific linguistic, cultural,
ecological, and institutional environments of this hemisphere, built on
shared principles and shared infrastructure, but locally meaningful in
ways that externally-built systems are not.

That is what GLAPAGOS is building toward.
